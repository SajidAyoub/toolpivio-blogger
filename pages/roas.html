How to use this tool

  1. Enter campaign revenue attributed using one consistent attribution model.
  2. Enter advertising spend for the same campaign and reporting period; spend must be greater than zero.
  3. Calculate to see the revenue multiple and percentage. Reset clears the form for another campaign.

Formula and units

ROAS = attributed revenue ÷ advertising spend. ROAS percentage = ROAS × 100.

Worked example

A campaign generates 5,000 in attributed revenue from 1,000 in ad spend. Its ROAS is 5,000 ÷ 1,000 = 5×, equivalent to 500%.

What to check before using the result

ROAS is a revenue measure. It excludes product costs, refunds, agency fees, platform fees and overhead unless those are included in your source figures. A high ROAS does not establish that a campaign is profitable.

Is 5× ROAS the same as a 500% profit?

No. 5× ROAS means five units of revenue for each unit of advertising spend. Calculate profit after all relevant costs separately with the profit margin calculator.

How to calculate break-even ROAS

Break-even ROAS is the revenue you need per unit of ad spend to cover your ads and variable order costs. First, subtract product costs, shipping you pay, payment fees and expected returns from revenue to find your contribution before ads. Use the same revenue basis here and in your ad report.

Contribution margin before ads = (revenue − non-ad variable costs) ÷ revenue. Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin before ads, expressed as a decimal.

Example: a 40% contribution margin

An order brings in $100 and has $60 in non-ad variable costs. The $40 left can cover up to $40 in advertising. Your break-even ROAS is $100 ÷ $40 = 2.5×, or 250%. At 3× ROAS, ad spend is about $33.33 per $100 of revenue, leaving about $6.67 before fixed costs.

Use the Profit Margin Calculator to estimate the contribution margin by entering only your non-ad variable costs. If the margin is zero or negative, additional ad spend cannot make the order break even under these assumptions.

What is a good ROAS?

Compare your measured ROAS with your own break-even point. Set a higher target if the campaign also needs to cover fixed costs or contribute a specific profit. Compare like-for-like figures when reviewing Meta Ads and Google Ads, since the platforms may credit the same order differently.
